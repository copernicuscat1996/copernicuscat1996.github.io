THE STORY

Sometimes grief comes with a verdict.

Nic is the youngest of five. After his father's death, the grief he carries is overshadowed by accusations from the very family he hoped would understand him.

As blame turns into judgment, Nic is forced to face a painful question: how do you survive when the people who know your name refuse to know your truth?

PROLOGUE

The Day Everything Changed

Nic was at home when his father died.

That was the part nobody seemed to understand.

He wasn't standing beside his father's hospital bed.

He wasn't holding his father's hand.

He wasn't there when the doctors made the final announcement.

He wasn't there when his father's heartbeat stopped.

He was home.

With his partner.

They were taking down Christmas decorations.

It was supposed to be an ordinary day.

The holidays were ending, and they were putting away the lights, ornaments, and decorations that had made the house feel warm.

Nic remembered the small things.

The tangled Christmas lights.

The boxes they were trying to organize.

His partner laughing when one of the decorations wouldn't fit into its container.

It was such an ordinary moment.

A moment that would become unforgettable for a reason neither of them could have imagined.

Then the news came.

His father had died.

Nic froze.

For a moment, the words didn't make sense.

His father.

Gone.

Nic stared at his partner.

He couldn't speak.

His partner immediately knew something was wrong.

"Nic?"

Nic's eyes filled with tears.

"Pa..."

His voice broke.

"Papa is gone."

His partner didn't ask questions.

Didn't tell him to be strong.

Didn't tell him to stop crying.

He simply went to him and held him.

Nic collapsed into his arms.

The Christmas decorations were still scattered around the room.

The lights were still tangled.

The boxes were still open.

But none of it mattered anymore.

Nic cried.

He cried because he had lost his father.

He cried because he wasn't there.

He cried because he couldn't say goodbye.

And his partner held him through it.

"It's okay, Nic."

"Let it out."

"I'm here."

Nic couldn't stop crying.

That was the first night his partner saw just how deeply Nic's father meant to him.

CHAPTER ONE

The News

The days that followed became a blur.

Phone calls.

Messages.

People coming and going.

Arrangements.

Questions.

Funeral preparations.

Nic barely knew what day it was.

He was grieving in a way he couldn't explain.

Sometimes he cried.

Sometimes he became silent.

Sometimes he stared into space.

Sometimes he suddenly remembered something about his father and the tears returned.

He kept thinking about one thing:

"I wasn't there."

It haunted him.

He knew his father had been sick.

He knew his father had been receiving medical care.

He knew the doctors had done what they could.

He knew the death was caused by his father's medical condition.

But grief doesn't always listen to logic.

Nic kept wondering whether he should have been there.

Whether he should have said something.

Whether he should have called.

Whether his father had been afraid.

Whether his father had looked for him.

Whether his father had wanted to say goodbye.

Questions without answers became prisoners inside his mind.

His partner tried to remind him:

"Nic, you didn't know."

"You couldn't have known."

But Nic kept crying.

"I should have been there."

His partner held his hand.

"You loved him. That's what matters."

Nic nodded.

But his heart wasn't ready to believe it.

CHAPTER TWO

The Viewing

His father's body was brought home for viewing.

The house changed overnight.

Chairs filled the yard.

People came carrying flowers.

Relatives arrived from different places.

Friends came to pay their respects.

Neighbors stopped by.

People whispered prayers.

There were conversations everywhere.

But Nic barely heard them.

His father's coffin was there.

Close enough to touch.

Close enough to see.

But far too still.

Nic would stand near the coffin sometimes.

He would look at his father.

And the reality would hit him again.

"Dad is really gone."

He wanted to touch him.

Sometimes he couldn't.

Sometimes he stood there for several minutes without moving.

Sometimes he cried.

Sometimes he walked away because looking was too painful.

But Nic slowly began noticing something.

He was being watched.

Every movement.

Every expression.

Every tear.

Every time he stepped away.

Every time he returned.

Someone was always looking.

At first, Nic thought he was imagining it.

Then he noticed the whispers.

He would walk toward the kitchen.

Someone would look at him.

He would sit outside.

Someone would watch.

He would speak with a relative.

Someone would observe.

Even when he cried, he felt eyes on him.

It became impossible to grieve naturally.

Nic started wondering:

"Are they watching me because they think I don't care?"

He tried to stay near the coffin longer.

Then he felt watched.

He tried to cry.

Then he wondered whether people thought he was pretending.

He tried to remain calm.

Then he wondered whether they would say he wasn't grieving.

There was no right way to behave.

Whatever Nic did seemed capable of being interpreted against him.

CHAPTER THREE

The Youngest

Being the youngest had once meant something different.

When Nic was a child, being the youngest meant being protected.

Now it seemed to mean something else.

It meant being questioned.

Being watched.

Being blamed.

His siblings had their own grief.

Nic understood that.

They had lost their father too.

But their pain increasingly became directed toward him.

Sometimes one of them would look at him and say:

"Where were you?"

Nic would answer quietly.

"At home."

"Of course you were."

The tone hurt.

Nic didn't understand what they wanted him to say.

"I didn't know Dad was going to die."

"You should have known."

"How?"

Silence.

Then someone would walk away.

Nic was left standing there wondering what he had done wrong.

CHAPTER FOUR

The Eyes

The viewing lasted for days.

And Nic felt like he was living inside a courtroom.

Every movement was evidence.

Every tear was questioned.

Every moment of silence became suspicious.

If he cried, people watched.

If he didn't cry, people watched.

If he smiled briefly while talking to someone, he wondered whether someone would accuse him of being happy that his father was dead.

He became terrified of appearing normal.

Because grief had taught him that even normal behavior could be used against him.

One afternoon, Nic laughed quietly at something a relative said.

It lasted only a second.

Then he immediately stopped.

He looked toward his father's coffin.

Guilt flooded him.

"I'm sorry, Dad."

He felt like he had committed some terrible crime simply by smiling.

His partner later told him:

"Nic, you're allowed to breathe."

Nic shook his head.

"They'll think I'm pretending."

"Let them think whatever they want."

"They're my family."

His partner looked at him.

"And you're still a human being."

Nic didn't answer.

But those words stayed with him.

CHAPTER FIVE

The Final Day

The day came when his father would be taken to his final resting place.

Nic barely slept the night before.

He stared at the ceiling.

He thought about the hospital.

He thought about the phone call.

He thought about the viewing.

He thought about his father's face.

And he thought about everything he wished he could have said.

"I'm sorry I wasn't there."

"I love you."

"Thank you."

"I wasn't ready to lose you."

The morning came.

People gathered.

Prayers were said.

The coffin was carried.

Nic watched.

His heart felt like it was being torn apart.

This was the final goodbye.

There would be no more viewing.

No more chance to stand near his father.

No more pretending that maybe he would somehow wake up.

This was real.

His father was going to his final resting place.

Nic cried.

Not for attention.

Not for anyone watching.

He cried because the man he loved was being taken away for the last time.

And somewhere in his heart, he was still the youngest son who wanted his father to come home.

CHAPTER SIX

The Day After

The funeral was over.

The visitors were gone.

The flowers began to disappear.

The chairs were put away.

The house became quieter.

Nic thought perhaps he would finally have space to grieve.

He was wrong.

The confrontation came the next day.

Nic had barely recovered from the burial when his siblings approached him.

There was anger in their voices.

"We need to talk."

Nic looked at them.

"About what?"

One of them answered:

"About Dad."

Nic's stomach tightened.

"What about Dad?"

Then the accusations began.

"You weren't there when he died."

Nic swallowed.

"I know."

"You were at home."

"I didn't know he was going to die."

"You should have been there."

Nic's eyes filled with tears.

"I loved Dad."

Someone laughed bitterly.

"Loved him?"

Nic stared.

Then came the words that would stay with him for years.

"Your crying was fake."

Nic froze.

"What?"

"All those tears during the viewing? The funeral? The crying? It was all an act."

Nic couldn't breathe.

"That's not true."

"You just wanted people to feel sorry for you."

Another voice joined.

"You always make yourself the victim."

Nic shook his head.

"No..."

His voice became weak.

"I lost my father too."

But nobody listened.

"You weren't even there when he died."

"You should have done something."

"Everything happened because of you."

Nic stood there listening to people rewrite the worst days of his life.

His father's illness.

His hospitalization.

His death.

The funeral.

His grief.

Everything became his fault.

CHAPTER SEVEN

The Mother

Nic hoped his mother would defend him.

She didn't.

She was grieving.

Nic knew that.

He understood that losing her husband had destroyed her too.

But when she spoke, her words hurt even more.

"Maybe you should have done more."

Nic looked at her.

"Mom..."

"Your father was sick. You knew he was sick."

"I knew."

"Then why weren't you there?"

Nic struggled to answer.

"I didn't know he was going to die that day."

His mother looked away.

"I don't know anymore."

Nic felt something collapse inside him.

He had wanted his mother's arms.

He wanted comfort.

He wanted to hear:

"It isn't your fault."

Instead, he heard doubt.

And doubt from a mother can feel heavier than hatred from anyone else.

Nic walked away.

He entered his room.

Closed the door.

And cried.

But now he was afraid even of crying.

Because what if they called those tears fake too?

CHAPTER EIGHT

The Violence

The arguments didn't stop.

They became more frequent.

Sometimes Nic was verbally attacked.

Sometimes he was cornered.

Sometimes anger became physical.

Nic sustained injuries.

Bruises.

Pain.

Damage that he tried to hide.

He learned to cover himself.

Long sleeves.

Quiet explanations.

"I bumped into something."

But the truth was that he was being hurt by people he had grown up with.

People whose names were connected to his childhood.

People who had once laughed with him.

People who once shared meals with him.

Now they were capable of making him afraid.

And the most painful part was that Nic still loved them.

That confused him.

"How can I love people who hurt me?"

His partner answered:

"Because love doesn't disappear just because someone hurts you."

"But should I still love them?"

"You don't have to stop loving them."

"But you do have to protect yourself."

Nic cried.

That was the first time someone had told him that protecting himself wasn't betrayal.

CHAPTER NINE

Leaving

Eventually, Nic understood that staying was destroying him.

He needed to leave.

He found work far away.

The decision frightened him.

But the fear of staying was greater.

He packed his belongings.

He took his documents.

His clothes.

His essentials.

And his father's photograph.

Before leaving, Nic stood in the house one final time.

He looked around.

This was the place where he had grown up.

But it was no longer the place where he felt safe.

He whispered:

"I'm sorry."

He wasn't sure who he was apologizing to.

His mother.

His siblings.

His father.

Or himself.

Then he left.

CHAPTER TEN

A Life Measured in Pesos

Far away from home, Nic discovered another kind of hardship.

Financial survival.

Every paycheck had a destination.

Rent.

Food.

Transportation.

Bills.

Work expenses.

Emergency savings.

He learned how to stretch every peso.

He couldn't afford to waste money.

Sometimes he wanted something simple.

A new shirt.

A meal somewhere nice.

Something just for himself.

But he would calculate the cost.

"Maybe next payday."

There were times he ate cheaply because he needed his money to last.

Times when he worked while exhausted.

Times when he looked at his finances and wondered how he would get through another month.

But he continued.

Because at least the struggle was his own.

Nobody was standing over him telling him he was guilty.

Nobody was watching his tears.

Nobody was telling him how he was supposed to grieve.

CHAPTER ELEVEN

The Distance

Distance didn't erase Nic's pain.

Sometimes it made it louder.

At night, he would remember everything.

The hospital.

The phone call.

His father's coffin.

The viewing.

The eyes watching him.

His siblings' accusations.

His mother's words.

The physical pain.

The emotional pain.

Everything.

Sometimes Nic would cry alone.

Then his partner would call.

"Are you okay?"

Nic would say:

"I'm fine."

His partner knew better.

"You don't have to be fine with me."

That sentence became one of the safest things Nic had ever heard.

CHAPTER TWELVE

The People Who Stayed

Nic's partner became his anchor.

Not physically.

Distance prevented that.

But mentally and emotionally, they were there.

And their friends were there too.

They checked on Nic.

They talked to him.

They listened.

They encouraged him.

They reminded him that his life was bigger than what his family said about him.

When Nic doubted himself, they reminded him of who he was.

When Nic felt alone, they stayed.

When Nic couldn't find words, they waited.

They didn't demand that he heal quickly.

They simply wanted him to keep going.

CHAPTER THIRTEEN

The Darkest Night

Eventually, the accumulated weight became almost unbearable.

Nic had lost his father.

He had been blamed.

He had been physically hurt.

He had been emotionally destroyed.

He was financially struggling.

He was far from home.

He missed his father.

He missed the idea of having a family that felt safe.

And one night, everything became too much.

Nic found himself thinking about whether life was worth continuing.

The thought scared him.

He didn't want to become a tragedy on top of another tragedy.

He didn't want his father to be followed by another loss.

But pain can make a person forget everything except the pain itself.

Then his partner called.

Nic didn't answer.

They called again.

And again.

Eventually, Nic answered.

There was silence.

Then his partner said:

"Nic... just stay with me."

Nic began crying.

His partner didn't lecture him.

Didn't shame him.

Didn't tell him to be stronger.

They simply stayed.

Friends reached out too.

One message.

Then another.

Then another.

"We're here."

"Please don't face this alone."

"Talk to us."

"You matter."

And slowly, Nic began talking.

He told them how exhausted he was.

How much pain he had been hiding.

How he didn't know how to carry it anymore.

That night, Nic didn't solve his life.

But he did something more important.

He stayed alive.

And he allowed people to help him.

CHAPTER FOURTEEN

After the Dark

The next morning wasn't magically better.

Nic still hurt.

His father was still gone.

His family was still complicated.

His past was still there.

But Nic was still here too.

His partner reminded him:

"You don't have to fix your whole life today."

"Just get through today."

So Nic did.

Then tomorrow came.

He got through that too.

Slowly, he learned to break life into smaller pieces.

One day.

One meal.

One shift.

One bill.

One conversation.

One night.

One morning.

He stopped demanding that himself be completely healed.

Instead, he focused on staying.

CHAPTER FIFTEEN

The Past Keeps Calling

Even after leaving, Nic's siblings sometimes brought the past back.

A phone call.

A message.

A conversation.

His name would be mentioned.

And suddenly the old accusations returned.

"You remember what happened to Dad."

Nic would close his eyes.

He remembered.

How could he not?

But he no longer argued the way he used to.

He would simply say:

"I remember Dad."

And sometimes:

"I remember what happened to me too."

Because Nic finally understood that both things could be true.

He could love his father.

And he could acknowledge that his family hurt him.

He could grieve.

And he could protect himself.

He could forgive someday.

And still maintain distance today.

CHAPTER SIXTEEN

The Photograph

One evening, Nic sat alone with his father's photograph.

He stared at it for a long time.

"Dad..."

He smiled sadly.

"I wasn't there."

His eyes filled with tears.

"I'm sorry."

He paused.

"But I loved you."

He looked down.

"I hope you knew that."

The room was silent.

Then Nic said:

"They say my tears were fake."

His voice broke.

"But you know they weren't."

He wiped his face.

"I miss you."

And for the first time, Nic didn't feel ashamed of crying.

He cried because he loved his father.

There was nothing fake about that.

CHAPTER SEVENTEEN

Becoming His Own Family

Nic slowly began building something new.

Not a replacement for his family.

Something different.

A life where he could make decisions without fear.

A life where he could rest.

A life where he could work toward something.

A life where his partner and trusted friends could be part of his support system.

He continued budgeting.

He continued working.

He saved what he could.

He learned to take care of himself.

He learned that surviving financially was also a form of strength.

Every month he managed to pay his bills was a victory.

Every time he saved even a little money was a victory.

Every time he survived a difficult night was a victory.

Every time he chose to stay was a victory.

CHAPTER EIGHTEEN

What His Father Left Behind

Nic eventually realized his father had left him more than memories.

He had left lessons.

Work hard.

Be responsible.

Don't give up.

Take care of yourself.

Love your family.

And perhaps most importantly:

Keep going.

Nic had spent years thinking his father's death was the thing that destroyed him.

But maybe his father's life was also the thing that taught him how to rebuild.

The pain would always be part of Nic.

But it didn't have to control him.

CHAPTER NINETEEN

The Son Who Was Still Here

Nic was still the youngest of five.

Still someone's son.

Still someone's brother.

Still someone's partner.

But he was no longer the boy who stood silently while everyone blamed him.

He had learned to speak.

He had learned to walk away.

He had learned to protect himself.

He had learned that surviving abuse wasn't weakness.

He had learned that needing help wasn't weakness.

And he had learned something even more important:

His father's death did not make him guilty.

His father's illness wasn't his fault.

The hospital wasn't his fault.

The death wasn't his fault.

Not being at the hospital that day wasn't proof that he didn't love his father.

And crying at the viewing wasn't an act.

Those tears belonged to a son mourning his father.

EPILOGUE

The Son Who Chose to Stay

Years later, Nic still remembered the day his father died.

He remembered exactly where he was.

At home.

With his partner.

Taking down Christmas decorations.

He remembered the moment the news arrived.

The shock.

The tears.

The embrace.

He remembered the viewing.

The visitors.

The relatives.

The prayers.

And the eyes that followed him everywhere.

He remembered the burial.

The final goodbye.

And the day after.

The accusations.

"You should have done more."

"You weren't there."

"Everything was your fault."

"Your crying was fake."

He remembered the physical pain.

The emotional scars.

His mother's words.

The nights he wanted to disappear.

But he also remembered something else.

The people who stayed.

His partner.

The friends who reached out.

The voices that told him:

"Stay."

"We're here."

"You matter."

And Nic realized that his story wasn't about the family that broke him.

It was also about the people who helped him rebuild.

His father died because of a medical condition.

Nic could not change that.

He could not go back to the hospital.

He could not rewrite that day.

He could not stand beside his father at the moment of his death.

But he could honor his father by continuing to live.

So Nic kept working.

He kept budgeting.

He kept building.

He kept remembering.

He kept healing.

And when the memories became heavy, he no longer believed he had to carry them alone.

He would look at his father's photograph and say:

"Dad, I still miss you."

Then he would breathe.

"But I'm still here."

And that sentence became his quiet victory.

Because there was a time when Nic didn't know if he would make it this far.

There was a time when his family's words made him question his worth.

There was a time when grief made him feel like he didn't belong anywhere.

But he survived.

Not perfectly.

Not without scars.

Not without bad days.

He survived because someone reached for him when he couldn't reach for himself.

And eventually, he learned to reach back.

Nic never forgot his father.

He never forgot what happened.

But he stopped letting the worst day of his life become the definition of his entire life.

He was still the youngest son.

Still carrying memories.

Still carrying scars.

Still learning.

But now he understood something that took him years to learn:

He wasn't responsible for his father's death.

He wasn't responsible for other people's inability to process their grief.

And he wasn't required to destroy himself just because others couldn't forgive him for something he never did.

His father's death was part of his story.

His family's blame was part of his story.

His darkest night was part of his story.

But they were not the ending.

Nic was still writing.

And every morning he woke up was another page.

Another chance.

Another breath.

Another day to live.

And perhaps that was the greatest thing he could give his father.

Not another apology.

Not another tear.

Not another punishment.

But a life.

A life his father would have wanted him to live.

And so Nic kept going.

For his father.

For the people who loved him.

For the partner who never stopped believing in him.

For the friends who refused to let him disappear.

And finally...

for himself.
